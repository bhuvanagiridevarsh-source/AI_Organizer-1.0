# System Janitor — Product Hunt Launch Kit

---

## 1. Tagline (60 chars max) — ranked

**1. "Your files, organized by AI that never leaves your device"** (58 chars) — Recommended. Leads with the outcome, and the privacy differentiator lands in the same breath. "Never leaves your device" does more work than "local" for a general PH audience.

**2. "A local AI that cleans your messy folders. No cloud, ever."** (59 chars) — Punchier, more attitude. "No cloud, ever" is a strong hook, but "local AI" is jargon to some browsers.

**3. "The file organizer that doesn't need your willpower"** (52 chars) — Most original angle, best conversation starter, but buries the AI + privacy differentiators that make people click.

## 3. Maker's first comment (~180 words)

> Hey Product Hunt 👋
>
> I built System Janitor because my Downloads folder hit 4,300 files and every organization system I tried collapsed within weeks. The problem was never the system — it was that filing files is a chore that depends on willpower, and willpower loses.
>
> The obvious fix was AI, but I didn't want my invoices, contracts, and tax docs uploaded to someone's server to get sorted. So System Janitor bundles a small language model that runs entirely on your machine. Unplug your network cable — it still works. No account, no telemetry, no subscription. You buy it once.
>
> It's deliberately cautious: it previews every move before touching anything, keeps a full undo log, and parks files it's unsure about in a Review folder instead of guessing. The local model isn't GPT-smart, and I'd rather it admit that than silently misfile your stuff.
>
> I'll be here all day answering everything, including the unflattering questions.
>
> One for you: what's the file count in your Downloads folder right now? Highest number gets a free license.

(Word count: ~180. The "highest number wins a license" question is cheap to fulfill and reliably drives comments — people love confessing their mess.)

## 4. Topics/tags to select

Product Hunt lets you pick up to 3. Recommended:

## 5. Launch-day submission targets

Verify each one's current submission process the week before — these change.

1. **Hacker News — "Show HN"** — Post as `Show HN: System Janitor – AI file organizer that runs 100% locally`. HN is hostile to marketing but loves local-first + one-time-purchase software. Lead the text with the technical decision (why a bundled small model instead of an API) and the limitations. If it hits the front page, it'll dwarf PH traffic.
2. **Ben's Bites (AI newsletter)** — accepts tool submissions; a local-model consumer app is differentiated content for them among endless cloud AI wrappers.
3. **There's An AI For That (theresanaiforthat.com)** — the largest AI tool directory; steady long-tail traffic rather than a launch spike, but submit on launch day so listings compound.
4. **r/SideProject** — welcoming to launch posts (unlike most subs), and the "I built this because my Downloads folder was a disaster" story fits perfectly. Cross-link your PH page — allowed there.
5. **TLDR newsletter (tldr.tech)** — has a submission form for its links sections; huge dev/tech-professional readership that overlaps exactly with people who care about "no cloud."

Bonus if you have capacity: Peerlist Launchpad (a PH alternative with a friendlier indie crowd) and MacStories/MacMenuBar for the Mac side.

## 6. Suggested launch day

**Tuesday.**

Reasoning: PH traffic peaks Tuesday–Thursday; launching then means competing harder for Top 5 but reaching far more actual buyers. Tuesday specifically gives you the full workweek for momentum — press and newsletters that pick up PH winners publish Wed–Fri, and your HN/Reddit cross-posts land while people are at their desks (where their messy folders are).

The alternative play — Sunday/Saturday — makes "Product of the Day" much easier because competition is thin, but the badge is worth less and total traffic can be 3–5x lower. Since System Janitor sells to a broad audience (anyone with a messy Downloads folder) rather than needing the badge for credibility, take the traffic.

Launch at 12:01 AM Pacific — PH days run midnight to midnight PT, and launching at the reset gives you the full 24 hours to accumulate votes.

## Consistency check

This kit uses **one-time purchase, no subscription** per your brief. Your Reddit beta posts floated ~$10/mo as unconfirmed. Before launch day, lock the pricing story — PH commenters will find the Reddit threads, and "he said one-time on PH but subscription on Reddit" is exactly the kind of thing that derails a launch thread. If you've settled on one-time, go back and update the Reddit comments to say so.
